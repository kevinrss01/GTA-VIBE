# Gun shop, the money system, and the interior fit-out

## What this covers

- `gunStore`, a new interior kind, placed on an existing enterable parcel.
- The shopkeeper, the generated weapons on show, and the counter interface.
- The cash readout on the HUD.
- Generated furnishings shared across every enterable interior.

The economy itself is **not** here. Money, weapons, ammunition and the wanted
level all live in `src/player/PlayerState.ts`, which this feature reads and
writes and never duplicates.

## Where the shop is, and why there

`parcel-69`, Old Quarter, on Vestry Street: 12.1 x 11.8 m, facing south, door
at `(-7.656, 21.0)`.

It is one of the two shopfronts the plan already promoted in that district, not
a new building. `ENTERABLE_TARGETS` in `CityPlan.ts` used to ask for
`{ oldQuarter, store, count: 2 }`; it now asks for `{ store, 1 }` followed by
`{ gunStore, 1 }`. Splitting one target into two draws the same parcels in the
same order from the same RNG stream, so **no other building in the city moved**
— the cafe, lobby, stairhall, workshop and market hall are on exactly the
parcels they were on before, and the second Old Quarter shopfront simply
changed trade.

Grounding is untouched: the shop uses the plan's existing doorway, plinth and
finished floor level, and all seven enterable parcels still enter at
delta 0.000 m.

### Fitting: the dominant axis is not the same for every model

Every one of these arrives normalised into a unit box, so exactly one
dimension is 0.998 — and which one differs per model. Fitting a sofa whose
0.998 is its LENGTH by its height produces a sofa two and a half metres tall.
`FURNISHING_SPECS` therefore names the axis whose real size is known and the
runtime divides by the model's own proportion on that axis.

Two of them are deliberately NOT fitted by their dominant axis, because the
model's proportions differ from the real object's and the dimension that must
be right is the other one:

- `bistro-table` is proportionally wider than a real cafe table (1.19:1 against
  0.96:1). Fitted by its diameter it is 0.59 m tall, which reads as broken next
  to a 0.90 m chair. Fitted by height it is 0.88 m across, which nobody
  notices. Height wins.
- `counter-till` is a whole counter with a till on it, and its length would put
  the top at 1.36 m. Fitted by overall height it is a 1.23 m long trade desk.

`front` — the model's own local axis that points forward — was measured rather
than guessed: for each mesh the half of the bounding box with the higher mean
vertex height is the BACK (a backrest, a stall's header board, a machine's
body), so the other half is the front. That is what stops every sofa being
placed with its back to the room.

Prompts, `face_limit`, and the downloaded previews are preserved under
`tripo-out/`. Source artefacts stay there; only the converted runtime GLB is
copied into `public/`.

All four arrive normalised into a unit box with a CENTRE pivot, so none is
usable as delivered.

`src/shop/WeaponDisplay.ts` re-scales the two weapons to a real length in
metres (rifle 1.12 m, pistol 0.21 m) and moves the origin to the centre of its
own bounding box, because every placement rotates the model and a
footprint-corner origin drifts under rotation.

`src/shop/Furnishings.ts` takes the other route for the plant and the chair:
`ModelLibrary` already normalises to a target HEIGHT with the origin at the
centre of the footprint at floor level, so loading both at exactly one metre
makes a piece's real height (plant 1.34 m, armchair 0.84 m) a plain scalar in
the instance matrix and keeps its feet on the ground.

Two orientation facts were established by looking at the running game, not
derived from the axes:

- the rifle's local **+X is the butt**, so standing one in a rack needs
  `rotateZ(-PI/2)`; `+PI/2` stands every rifle on its muzzle;
- the pistol already stands on its magazine base with the barrel along local
  `+Z`, so it only needs a yaw.

## The shop

### Interior

`gunStore` is a full interior kind with its own palette and layout
(`interiorProps.ts`). The fit-out is:

- a service counter across the room with a glazed display case, a till, a card
  terminal, drawer lines and a rubber duckboard behind it;
- a low cabinet run the width of the back wall with a stone top;
- a dark rifle rack over it: boarded panel, butt tray, muzzle dividers, a
  retaining rail and price tickets;
- pegboards either side of the rack hung with accessories;
- a gunsmith's bench in the staff strip with a vice, a parts tray and a tool
  board;
- four runs of boxed ammunition down both flanks under lit header boards;
- a tall glazed accessory cabinet, a gun safe, an island of hard cases, a
  pallet of stock, a bin of cleaning rods;
- framed licence, safety notices, a paper target and one lit shop board;
- four strip lights, which is what the `store` it replaced also asked for, so
  the city's interior light count is unchanged.

The counter is deliberately set **behind** the middle of the room:
`blocksEntry` treats the centre of the floor as the end of the walk-in route,
so a counter laid across it would be refused outright.

### The clerk

`src/shop/Shopkeeper.ts` draws one instance of a baked crowd character
(`ped-c`) playing its idle clip, placed behind the counter facing the customer.

This reuses the Tripo-generated, auto-rigged, retargeted characters the crowd
already ships, along with their vertex-animation shader, their material and
their already-compiled program. It costs one instanced draw call and no
credits, and — unlike a static generated figure — the clerk actually breathes.

### The counter interface

`src/ui/GunShopUi.ts`. Opens on the interaction point at the customer side of
the counter, shows cash and every weapon, what is owned and how many rounds are
carried, and offers two actions per row: buy the weapon, or buy a magazine for
it. Arrow keys move, Enter buys, Escape leaves. It owns no state; it reads
`PlayerState.snapshot()` and calls `buyWeapon` / `buyAmmo`.

### The display case

The counter used to be a price list: four rows of four numbers each, and a
player expected to hold twelve of them in their head to compare the fifth.
Above the list there is now a display case.

`src/shop/WeaponPreview.ts` puts the SELECTED weapon's real GLB on a slow
turntable in a 300x214 panel, and `src/shop/weaponStats.ts` puts five stats
beside it as bars: damage per trigger pull, rate of fire, accuracy, range and
magazine. Every fraction is the weapon's own value over the BEST value in the
catalogue for that stat, so a full bar means nothing here beats it, and the
real quantity is printed beside every bar in its real unit. Each of the four
weapons wins exactly one row, which is a property of the armoury and is
asserted as one in `tests/weapons.test.ts`.

Selection follows FOCUS. The arrow keys that already moved between rows now
also turn the weapon on the stand, and clicking anywhere on a row does the
same; there is no second thing to learn. Because a browser suppresses focus
events entirely while its own window is in the background - which is the state
an automated harness drives the game in - the arrow-key handler selects
explicitly rather than relying on `focusin` alone.

The preview owns its OWN small `WebGLRenderer`. Borrowing the game's would mean
a scissored second pass, a camera swap and a render-target copy into the DOM on
every frame the counter is open; a second context on a 300-pixel canvas is one
extra context out of the sixteen a browser allows. It carries NO POINT LIGHT:
the metal reads because the scene has a pre-filtered environment baked once
from a procedural two-softbox gradient painted into a 128x64 canvas, plus one
directional light for the highlight down the barrel. It renders only while the
counter is open, driven from `GunShop.update` - the game's own clock, not a
private `requestAnimationFrame`, so it stops when the game stops and steps when
an automated harness steps the game by hand.

The Dock Sweeper's cobalt-blue generation artefact is corrected here with the
same `tint` the held weapon uses, so the shop cannot sell a blue shotgun and
then hand the player a grey one.

## Generated furnishings in the other interiors

The user's second complaint was that the interiors were "too empty and a bit
too slapdash". Twelve generated models answer it, placed at 36 anchors across
all seven enterable buildings:

| room | what is in it |
| --- | --- |
| cafe | three bistro tables, six bentwood chairs, the espresso machine on the counter, two plants |
| store | drinks cabinet, two stock racks, a plant |
| gun shop | a plant (its own fit-out is already the densest in the city) |
| lobby | reception sofa, two armchairs, drinks cabinet, two plants |
| stairhall | armchair, drinks cabinet, two plants |
| workshop | two workbenches, stock rack, trade counter, armchair |
| market hall | two produce stalls, two plants |

**The cafe's seating is now generated, not built.** The layout used to make a
pedestal of boxes with two more boxes for chairs; `addCafeFittings` no longer
emits any of it, and `cafeSeats(room)` hands the anchors to the furnishing
pass instead. A cafe chair is the one object in a room like this that nobody
accepts as a cuboid. The counter's own position had to be made free of `rng`
for this (`cafePlan`), for the same reason `gunStorePlan` is: the espresso
machine stands ON that counter and is placed by a module that cannot share the
room's random stream, so a counter drawn from `rng` would have put the machine
beside it about half the time.

`interiorFurnishings(parcel)` in `interiorProps.ts` decides where each piece
stands and is free of `rng`, because the world build and
`src/world/furnishings/Furnishings.ts` both call it and must agree. Anchors
carry a QUARTER-TURN count rather than a free angle, so a piece's footprint
stays axis-aligned in the room frame and its collider can be the exact
rectangle it occupies - a two-metre sofa inside a two-metre square collider
blocks a metre of floor that is not there. The build emits a trip-height collider at each
anchor; the runtime places the mesh. Anything that would stand in the walk-in
route is dropped by the same `canPlace` check the rest of the fit-out uses, and
`tests/gunShop.test.ts` asserts the clearance independently.

On top of that every interior now gets a **dressing pass**: framed pictures and
notices, letterboxes, a coat stand, a bin, a radiator, a rug, a low table. None
of it is furniture and none of it takes a collider. It is the layer a room
reads as missing long before it reads as short of a chair.

Interior point lights are also spent at the top of the band the budget allows
rather than the middle (`LIGHT_LIFT` in `interiorProps.ts`). The COUNT is
unchanged - 28 across the city, as before - and `tests/interiors.test.ts` still
holds intensity to 1.4-2.4 and distance to 8-14 m. The reason is measured: an
interior has no sun, so an up-facing surface away from a fitting is lit almost
entirely by the hemisphere's pale blue sky term, and warm timber floors were
rendering as navy.

Per-interior triangles after the change: cafe 2,144 (DOWN — its procedural
seating is gone); lobby 2,132; stairhall 3,176; gun shop 3,728; workshop
3,350; store 2,680; market hall 3,726.

The generated total is large but almost none of it is ever submitted: each
interior's furniture hangs under its own group and is switched off beyond
42 m, so at most one room is drawn at a time — 17,126 triangles in the worst
case (the cafe) across four draw calls. The gun shop's façade sign is the one
generated object that is never culled, because being visible from down Vestry
Street is its entire job.

Measured in the browser at 1280x720, pixel ratio 1.2, standing at the gun shop
counter with the clerk, nine rifles, six pistols and the furnishings drawn:
4.37 ms mean frame, 328 draw calls. The same build on the street outside:
5.34 ms. The shop costs nothing measurable.

The shop's generated stock and every room's furnishings are hidden entirely
beyond 42 m: they are inside buildings, nobody can see them from the next
street, and WebGL will not work that out on its own. Furnishings are grouped
per interior for exactly this reason - one mesh per model across the whole city
would have had a bounding sphere the size of Meridian Bay.

## Third pass: the market hall and the street

Ten more assets were generated by the coordinator and placed here. Five are
interior (`stall-fish`, `stall-butcher`, `stall-flowers`, `cafe-counter`,
`vending-machine`) and five are street furniture (`street-bench`,
`litter-bin`, `bus-shelter`, `phone-kiosk`, `news-stand`).

### The market hall is now generated end to end

`addMarketFittings` no longer builds stalls. Its `addStall`/`runStalls` pair -
a counter, a back board and a canopy of boxes, fifteen times - is gone, and
four generated trades (fish, butcher, flowers, produce) alternate down the same
three rows the procedural run used, so the paved aisle, the trusses and the
high bay lamps still line up with them. What stays in the builder is
everything a stall does not own: the shell, the aisle, the lighting, the hall
sign and the barrow. The hall's own geometry fell from 3,726 to 1,278
triangles doing it.

The rows are packed tighter than before (one stall every 4.8 m rather than
every 6.4 m) because a generated stall is about two metres of frontage where an
authored one was nearly four; at the old spacing the hall read as a row of
exhibits rather than a market.

`cafe-counter` did NOT replace the cafe's counter, which was the judgement
asked for. The procedural one carries the espresso machine, the grinder, the
till, a row of cups, the back bar and four stools, and the model is a bare
counter with a display case; swapping it in would have removed six things to
add one. It went to the market hall instead, as the refreshment counter every
hall of this kind has - a real home rather than a downgrade.

### Street furniture

`src/world/furnishings/StreetProps.ts` replaces prop geometry the same way
`main.ts` already replaces the generated street lamp: `createPropGeometry`
authors a procedural version of every prop, and anything that downloads
overwrites its entry in the table before the city is baked. The props stay one
`InstancedMesh` per material key per chunk, so they keep chunk distance
culling and shadow behaviour for free, and a prop that fails to download falls
back to its authored massing.

`bench` and `litterBin` are replaced outright. `busShelter`, `phoneKiosk` and
`newsStand` are new prop types - three additive entries in `PropKey` and
`ALL_PROP_KEYS`. `newsBox` was deliberately NOT replaced by `news-stand`: a
0.55 x 0.47 m box and a 1.22 x 1.52 m kiosk are different objects, and
enlarging thirteen existing sites would have pushed on the doorway-clearance
rule for no gain.

Two measurements drove the sizes:

- The **bench got smaller**. 1.90 m of authored slats became a 1.40 m
  two-seater, because the model is proportionally taller and its seat height is
  the dimension that has to be right. A smaller collider is strictly safer for
  pavement corridors.
- The **bin kept its footprint exactly**, fitted by WIDTH rather than height,
  because every pavement clearance rule in the city is measured against that
  number.
- The **shelter's collider is its back wall and posts, not its volume**. A
  shelter is something people stand inside; boxing it would have put a
  2.9 x 1.5 m wall across a footway. It is also 2.85 m wide rather than the
  3.1 m the model would give at a 2.55 m roof, because `tests/streets.test.ts`
  holds every prop collider under three metres.

## Wiring

`GunShop` and `Furnishings` are wired. `StreetProps` is NOT, and until it is
the city draws the procedural massing for the bus shelter, the phone kiosk and
the news stand, and the authored bench and bin rather than the generated ones.
One block, next to the existing street-lamp override and BEFORE `sink.bake`:

```ts
import { loadStreetPropModels } from './world/furnishings/StreetProps';

const streetModels = await loadStreetPropModels(import.meta.env.BASE_URL);
for (const [key, parts] of streetModels.parts) propGeometry.set(key, parts as PropPart[]);
```

The rest of the contract, already applied, is at the top of
`src/shop/GunShop.ts` and `src/world/furnishings/Furnishings.ts`:

```ts
import { GunShop } from './shop/GunShop';
import { Furnishings } from './world/furnishings/Furnishings';
import { PlayerState } from './player/PlayerState';

const player = new PlayerState();
const shop = new GunShop({
  plan, player,
  baseUrl: import.meta.env.BASE_URL,
  onSound: () => audio.playOneShot('ui-tick'),
});
engine.scene.add(shop.group);
document.body.appendChild(shop.element);
shop.onOpenChange = (open) => {
  controller.setPaused(open);
  if (open) document.exitPointerLock();
  else controller.requestPointerLock();
};
player.onChange = () => { hud.setMoney(player.money); shop.refresh(); };
hud.setMoney(player.money);
void shop.load();

const furnishings = new Furnishings(plan, import.meta.env.BASE_URL);
engine.scene.add(furnishings.group);
void furnishings.load();
```
